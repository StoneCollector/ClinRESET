ClinRESET — MEDICAL ABBREVIATION / ACRONYM TERMINOLOGY CORPUS
===============================================================

Purpose:
  Reference corpus for Phase 3/4 terminology normalization and semantic
  structuring in the ClinRESET clinical-report simplification project.

IMPORTANT:
  This is a broad reference corpus assembled from authoritative web sources,
  not a claim of exhaustive coverage of every abbreviation ever used in
  medicine. Medical abbreviations are context-dependent and facility-specific.

SOURCE SET USED
---------------
1. U.S. National Library of Medicine / MedlinePlus, Appendix B:
   Common Abbreviations
   https://medlineplus.gov/appendixb.html

2. Merck Manual Consumer Version, Abbreviations:
   https://www.merckmanuals.com/home/resourcespages/abbreviations

3. U.S. National Cancer Institute / SEER Training, Common Abbreviations (All):
   https://training.seer.cancer.gov/abstracting/additional/abbreviations/all.html

4. American Society of Echocardiography, Guideline for the Standardization
   of Adult Echocardiographic Reporting, Measurement Dictionary and
   abbreviations:
   https://www.asecho.org/

ENTRY FORMAT
------------
ABBREVIATION | EXPANSION | DOMAIN / CONTEXT

DESIGN RULES FOR IMPLEMENTATION
-------------------------------
- Preserve the original abbreviation exactly as encountered.
- Normalize matching case-insensitively, but retain original surface form.
- Do NOT blindly replace an abbreviation when it has multiple meanings.
- Ambiguous abbreviations must be resolved using report type, section,
  neighboring terms, anatomy, measurements, and local context.
- A single abbreviation may map to multiple candidate concepts.
- Never infer a clinical finding merely because an abbreviation is present.
- Never use the corpus to assign NORMAL/ABNORMAL/PRESENT/ABSENT status.
- Keep normalization separate from interpretation.
- Preserve provenance: original text, page, section, source span.
- Prefer report-specific terminology over generic terminology when the
  report type is known.
- For echocardiography, prefer the ASE terminology where applicable.
- Do not automatically expand medication/dosing abbreviations in a way that
  could alter clinical meaning; keep the source abbreviation and normalized
  expansion separately.
- Treat one-letter abbreviations (A, P, R, T, U, etc.) as highly ambiguous.
- Treat symbols, units, and dose designations separately from clinical
  abbreviations.
- The Joint Commission "Do Not Use" list is a SAFETY resource, not an
  expansion dictionary. Its entries should be stored separately as
  prohibited/error-prone forms.

JOINT COMMISSION SAFETY NOTE
----------------------------
The Joint Commission identifies prohibited/error-prone abbreviations and
dose designations including:
  U/u, IU, QD/q.d./Q.D., QOD/q.o.d./Q.O.D., trailing zero (X.0 mg),
  missing leading zero (.X mg), MS, MSO4, MgSO4.
These must NOT be silently normalized as if they were unambiguous medical
concepts. They should trigger an ambiguity/safety flag where appropriate.
